Design-space explorer

The atlas, in excerpts

The repository's design-space atlas covers 30 cells — five rank classes (4, 8, 16, 32, 64 ranks) × six algorithm families (ring, crossbar, torus, recursive-doubling, phase collectives, L0.5) — each with exact-fraction tables like the ones below. Every number is the output of a closed-form analytical identity under explicit free parameters: evidence level E0, status PROPOSED, nothing measured, nothing standard-derived. Full tables in the repository (docs/atlas/).

Why the cells are comparable

Shared conventions (the fairness rule)

  • Message grid — the same 12 sizes in every cell: 4 KiB → 64 MiB as a maximally even power-of-two ladder (exponents {12,13,14,16,17,18,20,21,22,24,25,26}). Every size is divisible by 4096, hence by every rank count here, so the divisibility guards never reject a grid point and crossovers are found on a common grid.
  • Free parameters equal across algorithms — link bandwidth B = 64 bytes/ns and hop latency L = 200 ns in every topology file; ranks fixed per rank class; W1 Dense TP Decode shape (layers 2 × steps 3 = 6 AllReduce ops). The L0.5 family's extra parameter, the injection rate I, is fixed by the same rule at I = 64 bytes/ns.
  • Ring is the baseline — within every rank class, each cell reports deltas of its identities against the ring identity at the same payload size.
  • Provenance — one sweep manifest per cell; tables content-addressed under results/sweeps/<sweep_id>/; identical manifest + code reproduce byte-identical tables, and each cell's bar includes a deterministic rerun.

Cell 8r-crossbar

A pure translate, no crossover

The single-port crossbar at 8 ranks has the same traffic identity as the ring (7M/4 per rank — the crossbar buys no traffic reduction under this L0 model); the two completion-time identities differ only in the constant (2L = 400 ns vs 14L = 2800 ns), so the crossbar is faster at every finite payload and the delta never changes sign.

Cell 8r-torus (2×4)

The crossover

The 2×4 torus pays more wire than the ring (5M/2 per rank = 10/7 of the ring's 7M/4) for a shorter latency chain (8L vs 14L). The two identities cross exactly once, off-grid at M = L·B·r = 102400 B (100 KiB) — between the 64 KiB and 128 KiB points below: the torus wins below, the ring wins above. The delta identity is (3/7)(M − 102400)/(M + 102400).

Cell 64r-L0.5

The compute term, positive deltas

At 64 ranks, the L0.5 serialized-ring model adds an injection (serialization) term on top of the L0 identity: t = 63M/1024 + 25200 = tL0(2M) exactly, wire unchanged at 63M/32. It is the only landed family with positive deltas against the ring baseline at every rank class; the I→∞ limit is the baseline. Delta closed form: M/(M + L·B·r). Beyond the excerpts

What the full atlas adds

  • Every cell page records its sweep id, input hashes, determinism bar (byte-identical rerun), conservation checks, and any crossover arithmetic — plus recorded skips (configurations that cannot be generated, recorded honestly and never forced).
  • Cross-cell laws landed as exact-fraction facts on fresh executions: the ring balance ladder M* = L·B·r (50/100/200/400/800 KiB across rank classes), the torus factorization law and its multi-way concurrent crossovers (up to a four-way point at 64r), the crossbar envelope law −(r−2)/(r−1), and the L0.5 half-saturation shift law delta2r(M) = deltar(M/2).
  • Full tables in the repository (docs/atlas/) — index at docs/atlas/README.md, 30 cell pages, approved as E0 product content (2026-10-29).
