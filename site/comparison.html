Placement

Where essun sits

essun occupies a narrow crossing: it targets scale-up GPU fabrics for LLM inference, and it treats the specification, the multi-fidelity comparison, and the evidence discipline as the product itself. General-purpose simulators — ns-3, OMNeT++, the HTSim lineage — model far more of networking than essun attempts, and essun does not replace them; two of its discrete-event backends are pinned, credited external simulators executed under one manifest and one comparison gate. What essun adds around those engines is a discipline layer: anchored standard profiles, free parameters that stay visibly free, content-addressed run ids, byte-identical replay, and an evidence label on every claim.

If the question is whole-network behavior, production configuration correctness, or protocol breadth, the tools above are the right ones. If the question is comparing scale-up fabric options for inference collectives — with every emitted number traceable to a run record — that is the space essun works in, as a complement to the general-purpose simulators rather than a replacement.
