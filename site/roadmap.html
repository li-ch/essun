Where this is going

Vision and roadmap

Fabric decisions in AI infrastructure are still made from vendor slides and one-off simulators. We want to end that. ESSUN's answer is a portable contract between four things: what a specification says, what an implementation may choose, what a simulator actually models, and what the resulting evidence supports. One experiment manifest drives every fidelity, every emitted number traces to a content-addressed run record, and every claim carries an evidence label — so fabric decisions can be answered with numbers anyone can re-derive.

The strategy is profile-led and engine-plural: profiles, semantic bindings, and evidence records should matter more than which engine executes them, and a consortium can own its official profile while the common substrate stays neutral. A project becomes infrastructure only when others depend on it and can maintain it — so this roadmap is an experiment in earning institutional dependence, with falsifiable exit conditions at every phase, not a countdown to inevitability. The end game: scale-up fabric evolution becomes evidence-driven, the way model development became benchmark-driven. If the institutional thesis fails, the honest fallback is a valuable research comparison tool.

The strategy

Profile-led, engine-plural

ESSUN's strongest position is not another simulator competing for attention. It is the lowest-cost credible way to turn a network specification into an evaluable engineering claim:

specification says → implementation may choose → simulator models → evidence supports

Anchored requirements, explicit free parameters, one manifest, reproducible runs, multiple engines, and honest evidence labels are the pieces of that contract. What is missing — and what the roadmap below is built to earn — is external dependence: independent users, executed calibration, distributed maintenance, and a normative profile that changes simulation outputs rather than documenting them.

Three commitments follow. The decisive constituency is a small coalition of independent evaluation teams, including at least one infrastructure buyer — not consortium endorsement. The pitch to a working group is ambiguity exposure and executable examples, never a vendor ranking. And the core stays neutral: a consortium can own its official profile without owning the cross-standard substrate.

Future tense, on purpose

The roadmap in five phases

Time ranges are aspirations measured from public launch. A phase passes when its exit conditions are met with evidence — never because its deadline arrived.

Phase 0 · now → launch

Make the thesis testable. License review for everything published; honest capability and evidence matrices; breadth frozen to one workload and one semantic wedge; the profile contract with per-requirement dispositions; a small UEC-linked behavioral or constraint path implemented; independent launch testers recruited; upstream maintainers contacted about adapter boundaries.

Exit conditions

  • the semantic slice changes a tested observable, or rejects an invalid experiment
  • a deliberately wrong binding is caught
  • two outsiders run the small example without private help

If not: launch as a disciplined experimental tool — not the stronger positioning.

Phase 1 · launch → month 6

Prove independent usefulness. A small, relevant subset of the calibration plan executed, including a held-out case; independent reproductions; one real evaluation question supported; upstream fixes rather than forks; a stable versioned artifact and a concise technical account.

Exit conditions

  • two or more independent organizations reproduce relevant results
  • an external team reuses ESSUN for its own question
  • the semantic slice is verified within its declared scope
  • three or more calibration cases executed, one held out, limits published
  • three substantive external contributors; a second person can cut a release
Phase 2 · months 6–12

Form a credible coalition. Repeat use by two decision teams; a second normative profile once publication rights are resolved; multi-organization review; release and backend-update competence transferred; committed maintenance support; the paper written around demonstrated results; a bounded working-group pilot offered.

Exit conditions

  • two external teams use ESSUN repeatedly, at least one buyer-side
  • the second normative profile is legally distributable with an executable, tested scope
  • three people — two outside the owner's control — perform critical maintenance tasks
  • a year of support committed in money or staff time
  • a working group accepts a defined pilot, not a conversation
Phase 3 · months 12–18

Enter the specification production loop. A real draft or revision delta exercised with permission; ambiguities resolved through recorded review; release-candidate compatibility tests; a modest public reproduction round; neutral hosting only if operational commitments exist by then.

Exit conditions

  • a working group uses ESSUN in an actual revision process
  • a substantive interpretation or draft issue is resolved through that process
  • a profile update is produced by someone other than the owner
  • the owner's absence does not block a release
Phase 4 · months 18–24 and beyond

Make the default self-reproducing. The draft-to-profile workflow repeated across independent ecosystems; maintenance and profile ownership institutionalized; third parties initiating profiles without owner-led persuasion.

Exit conditions

  • new-profile work begins outside the owner's initiative
  • multiple specification organizations use the contract
  • maintenance survives personnel turnover

No honest plan promises this phase within two years. The two-year test is whether the mechanism works.

What success would actually look like

The measurable endgame

ESSUN is a de facto evaluation standard when relevant actors routinely assume its profile/evidence contract must be addressed — even if they choose a different engine or challenge the reference model. Concretely, all of the following would need to hold:

Release behavior
Across three or more successive specification-release events, spanning two or more independent ecosystems, third parties initiate ESSUN profiles or compatible updates — available at release or within a predefined short interval where publication rights permit, with at least two updates not owner-led.
Institutional behavior
Two or more working groups reference ESSUN in minutes, technical documents, or issue resolutions — actual technical use, not endorsement.
Contributor independence
Twelve or more substantive external contributors over a rolling year, across five or more organizations, with three or more non-owner maintainers of critical tasks; no single employer controls technical acceptance.
Engineering behavior
Engineers from three or more vendors file substantive issues or corrections; two or more independent infrastructure buyers use the substrate repeatedly; backend maintainers can implement against a published compatibility contract.
Research behavior
In a representative rolling sample of cross-fabric evaluation papers, at least half use the contract or explicitly compare against it.

What does not count as adoption

Stars, followers, site visits, supportive messages, cosmetic pull requests, owner-assisted demonstrations, downloads, sponsor money, or an introductory working-group meeting. Confidential use counts only as a permissioned attestation or reviewed summary — never as unverifiable "major-company adoption".

Falsifiable, not inevitable

Three legitimate outcomes

If the loop closes

Double down on standard positioning

Justified when technical validity, repeated decision use, distributed maintenance, and draft-cycle integration reinforce each other.

If researchers stay, buyers don't

The research comparison tool

Justified when researchers repeatedly use and cite ESSUN but buyers and consortia do not depend on it — important, publishable, sustainable, at a narrower scope and budget.

If adoption does not follow

Stop the standard narrative

Justified when the software is useful but institutional adoption does not materialize. Let demonstrated demand set the scope; do not manufacture inevitability through branding.

The decisive milestone is not launch, a foundation, a paper, or an endorsement. It is an independent team making a repeated decision on a causally executable, appropriately calibrated profile — and a second team able to reproduce, challenge, and improve it without the founder mediating everything.
