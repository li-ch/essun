FAQ:

Q1: What is agentic AI? Why does it have higher demands on computing architecture?

Answer: Agentic AI refers to AI systems that can autonomously plan, invoke other tools, and execute tasks multiple times independently. Compared with traditional generative AI, agentic AI requires more frequent data exchange between CPUs, NPUs, memory, and storage devices, thereby placing higher demands on communications efficiency and resource coordination.

Q2: What is KV Cache? What are Attention and FFN? What is the purpose of Attention-FFN disaggregation?

KV cache stores historical key-value pairs during AI model inference to avoid redundant computation. The longer the context, the more memory it consumes.

Attention is a mechanism that captures contextual relationships, while FFNs (Feed-Forward Networks) handle layer-by-layer feature transformation. They are both core computational modules of the Transformer architecture.

Attention-FFN disaggregation (AFD) decouples Attention and FFN and deploys them onto different compute units to enable flexible CPU-NPU mixes for different workloads. This improves inference efficiency and resource utilization. AFD is particularly valuable for agentic AI which requires frequent CPU-NPU collaboration.
