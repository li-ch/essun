Huawei Unveils New UnifiedBus Computing Architecture for SuperPoDs and Clusters

Summary:

Huawei has released a new computing architecture that utilizes UnifiedBus to support cluster and SuperPoD interconnect, in order to address booming compute demand.

Huawei launched a range of new UnifiedBus-powered devices that enable interconnection within cabinets, between cabinets, and across clusters, which unlocks elastic scaling from single cabinets to million-NPU clusters.

[Shanghai, China, September 17, 2026] In a keynote speech on computing architecture innovation at HUAWEI CONNECT 2026, Yang Chaobin, Huawei's Executive Director of the Board and CEO of the ICT Business Group, unveiled new details about the UnifiedBus interconnect architecture, announced a series of computing products the company has recently built based on this architecture, and explained how the company has collaborated with open-source communities to enable application innovation by customers, partners, and developers.

Yang Chaobin delivering a keynote at HUAWEI CONNECT 2026

In a traditional computing architecture, resource utilization declines as a cluster scales. As little as 20% of a 100,000-NPU cluster's computing capacity is actually utilized by models, with a vast amount of compute sitting idle during data communications. A 10 trillion-parameter model, which requires massive amounts of intermediate data to train, far exceeds the capacity of the memory on a single accelerator under traditional architectures. Furthermore, AI agents can have hundreds of interactions per hour with AI models, which means computing systems need to be able to flexibly utilize changing amounts of central processing units (CPUs) and neural processing units (NPUs). The resulting interactions between these processors also generate KV cache that far exceeds memory capacity, which is driving up demand for data storage across compute systems and tiers. All of this means interconnection and communications between components within a cluster have become a key performance bottleneck for computing systems.
