4.2.9.2.3 UET Congestion Control Use Cases 
A central problem in congestion control is prompt allocation of newly spare capacity on a link to flows 
that can use it. For example, when a flow releases its share of a bottleneck link, it is desirable for other 
flows that share the same bottleneck to rapidly claim the newly freed capacity. In this situation, a sender 
faces a trade-off. It can increase its rate slowly and conservatively by probing, which risks leaving 
capacity unused for multiple RTTs and lengthening flow completion time. Alternatively, it can increase 
its rate aggressively, and risk overshooting the spare capacity, which incurs congestive delay and packet 
losses. 
NSCC incorporates a fast_increase() mechanism that reclaims spare capacity aggressively when path 
queueing delay is modest and many ACKs have returned in the recent past without an ECN mark, both of 
which indicate that a path is lightly loaded. While fast_increase() successfully helps flows reclaim spare 
capacity rapidly, it is heuristic in nature in the sense that it is oblivious to the explicit measure of spare 
capacity at a bottleneck on a path: it increases a sender’s congestion window a constant two MTUs per 
returning ACK.
