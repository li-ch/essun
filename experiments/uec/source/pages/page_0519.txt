3.6.18.2 Congestion Management Algorithm Specification and Invocation 
Congestion management algorithms are a fundamental component of the UE PCM framework, 
responsible for dynamically regulating congestion through interactions with system signals and CC 
controls. They can be triggered by defined signals or timers, potentially allowing invocation for each 
incoming acknowledgment (ACK) packet to ensure prompt responsiveness to network conditions. These 
algorithms can both read and modify signals and controls, enabling adaptive congestion management 
strategies. 
At the time of algorithm invocation, the system captures a snapshot of all relevant signal values. These 
values remain fixed for the duration of the algorithm’s execution. Signals arriving after the invocation 
timestamp do not affect the current execution and are queued for processing during the next invocation 
cycle. 
Informative Text: 
PCM programs may assume that the algorithm is invoked immediately after the timed or control 
trigger occurs. Delays could lead to inconsistent signal observations and reduced algorithm fidelity. 
The exact details depend on the implementation. 
 
3.6.18.3 Load Balancing Algorithm Specification and Invocation 
Like congestion management algorithms, load balancing algorithms are invoked by data path transmit, 
ACK/NACK, and retransmit timeout events. Implementations may use triggers to modulate the 
frequency of these events on a per-CCC basis. LB algorithms are logically distinct from CC algorithms and 
are expressed as independent programs with their own entry point, accumulators, and triggers. 
PCM supports two distinct LB implementation models: per-packet and EV table.
