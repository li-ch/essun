2.4 In-Network Collectives (INC) 
High-performance computing (HPC) and artificial intelligence (AI) workloads frequently require multiple 
accelerators to coordinate as groups of cooperating processes that accomplish computational 
objectives. In the message-passing interface (MPI) and most collective communication libraries (CCLs), 
we refer to these groups as communicators, with each process in a group called a rank. Each rank 
receives an integer ID, ranging from 0 to r-1 for a group of r ranks. Ultra Ethernet (UE) adopts the same 
abstraction for the purpose of facilitating collective operations. 
Ultra Ethernet In-Network Collectives (INC) enables network switches to perform computational tasks 
directly for a range of group operations. These switches can accelerate key collective operations defined 
by the MPI and various CCLs. While this overview does not comprehensively list all such operations, the 
MPI specification has additional details. This INC specification focuses on a subset of collective 
operations from MPI and CCLs, demonstrating the adaptability and extensibility of the INC framework 
for high-performance computing environments. 
INC can reduce the bandwidth required for large-message reduction operations by a factor of two, as 
established in the literature [1]. This reduction in data movement immediately decreases energy 
consumption at the endpoints. By executing computations within the network, INC also lessens the 
computational burden and alleviates memory load on accelerators located at the endpoints. 
Additionally, INC can significantly reduce latencies for small operations, further enhancing performance 
and efficiency in high-performance computing environments. 
Collective operations are initiated by application software on a communicator. They are considered 
active from when the first call issues by a rank in the communicator until the final completion 
notification is received by a rank in the communicator. 
INC offers support to accelerate the following collective operations in switches: 
• Barrier 
• Broadcast 
• Allreduce 
• Reduce 
These basic operations can be used to accelerate more complex operations such as reduce-scatter. 
2.4.1 Assumptions 
UE makes several assumptions about the definition and deployment of INC operations: 
1. UE INC operates on a single logical switch with a single switch endpoint (SEP) in the network. 
Vendors may offer solutions where this single logical switch consists of a distributed set of 
switches, provided it adheres to the behavior specified herein. 
2. UE INC MUST operate over a lossless fabric (using PFC or CBFC) over the UET ROD transport 
between the FEP and the SEP. Each FEP MUST be directly connected to a port associated with a 
SEP.
