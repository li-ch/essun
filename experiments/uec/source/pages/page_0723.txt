5.4.6 UFH Termination and Forwarding 
UFH packets appear as legal L2 frames on the wire but cannot be forwarded by standard Ethernet 
bridges as defined in IEEE 802.1. A UFH-capable switch needs to mask out non-address portions of the 
DA and SA when implementing forwarding behaviors and support additional capabilities defined by the 
UFH header. A conceptual view of a UFH endpoint and switch is shown in Figure 5-28. 
The MAC layer at an endpoint identifies received UFH packets and forwards them to an upper layer 
through a “MAC Service Access Point.” The upper layer validates and terminates UFH packets addressed 
to the endpoint and passes them to the transport protocol identified in the header stack. 
Packets addressed to other endpoints are directed to a UFH switch that implements UFH-specific 
forwarding functions. The forwarding function looks up the UFH destination address to identify an 
egress interface, maps the encoded COS to a queue, and applies ECN and hop-limit updates analogous 
to those applied by an IP router. The forwarding function must ignore any opaque fields and reserved 
flags in the header. 
UFH defines 32-bit and 16-bit address container variants to support different deployment needs. These 
header variants are known as UFH_32 and UFH_16 respectively. While use of IP addresses is preferred 
with the 32-bit address variant, this specification intentionally does not mandate the use of a specific 
address family with either variant. The address fields are viewed as “containers” to which an 
implementation can map a suitable address namespace and conform to its forwarding rules. This 
flexibility is to help bespoke HPC and scale-up fabrics migrate to Ethernet. 
The address namespace used by an implementation might need fewer than 32 or 16 address bits, and 
the addresses must be LSB-aligned within an address container. UFH endpoints and switches MUST be
