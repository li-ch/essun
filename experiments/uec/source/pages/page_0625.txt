4.2.9.3.3 Point in Packet for Timestamping 
SOP indicates the start-of-packet event at a switch port. A UE CSIG-capable switch MUST measure the 
delay experienced by a packet by timestamping the packet at the SOP event at the ingress port and at 
the SOP at the egress port, then taking the difference between the latter timestamp and the former 
one. This approach does not include any extraneous delay proportional to a packet’s length in the 
measurement and is compatible with cut-through switch fabrics. A switch MAY subtract a fixed value 
from this measurement to account for the switch’s uncongested forwarding delay. 
4.2.9.3.4 UET Congestion Control Use Cases 
The max(Delay) signal is a trailing indicator of congestion: a packet must traverse a switch’s queue 
before a measurement of the delay incurred at that queue can be calculated. The value of this signal as 
an input to delay-based congestion control algorithms, such as NSCC, stems from its more targeted 
measurement of congestion than is possible with total end-to-end path delay. max(Delay) reveals the 
queueing delay experienced by a packet at a path’s single most constrained bottleneck, whereas whole-
path delay measurements, such as those used as input by NSCC, sum the queueing delays from all 
congested queues along a packet’s path. 
The more targeted congestion measurements enabled by max(Delay) can materially improve the 
bandwidth fairness of delay-based congestion control protocols. Previously published work [1]  
demonstrates that when two flows traverse the same congested queue, if one of the two flows 
encounters no other congested queues along its path and the other flow encounters one or more 
additional congested queues along its path, delay-based congestion control schemes that use whole-
path delay measurements exhibit bandwidth unfairness between the two flows. Such congestion control 
schemes reduce a flow’s sending rate in proportion to the amount that the flow’s total path delay 
exceeds a target path delay. 
Because the flow traversing multiple congested queues experiences greater total path delay, even 
though only a single queue along a path can be the most constrained bottleneck for any flow, its sending 
rate will be reduced proportionally more than that of the flow traversing only one congested queue. 
Poseidon, a delay-based congestion control algorithm that uses CSIG max(Delay)-like feedback [1] , 
improves fairness for such flow pairs by enabling the control loops for each of the two flows to respond 
to congestion only at that flow’s single true bottleneck (most congested) queue, such that the flow that 
traverses multiple congested queues is not disproportionately penalized. 
The max(Delay) signal should offer similar bandwidth fairness improvements to NSCC congestion 
control. Exploratory simulations of a version of NSCC modified to replace whole-path delay signals with 
CSIG max(Delay) signals demonstrate material bandwidth fairness improvements over baseline NSCC in 
scenarios in which multiple flows traverse a shared congested queue, while distinct flows among them 
traverse varying numbers of congested queues.
