4.2.9.1.2 Load Accounting 
UE-compliant switches MUST support a mode of operation in which they exclude MAC-generated 
frames in per-port transmit load accounting. min(ABW) measurements are intended to include only data 
frames subject to congestion control by an originating FEP. Other MAC-generated frames (e.g., PAUSE, 
LLR, CBFC, and PFC frames) SHOULD NOT be included in per-port transmit load accounting. Switches 
MAY support other variations of transmit load accounting outside the scope of this specification. 
4.2.9.1.3 UET Congestion Control Use Cases 
A potential use case for the min(ABW) signal in UE congestion control concerns improving the 
effectiveness of multi-path load balancing. Both the NSCC and RCCC congestion control algorithms 
incorporate multi-path load balancing, whereby a sending FEP selects and writes an entropy value (EV) 
in each packet it sends, and switches include this EV in the input to the hash functions they use for 
ECMP path selection. Distinct flows may obliviously select EVs that direct their traffic to collide at the 
same output port of a switch. When other uncongested paths are available, such flow collisions cause 
otherwise avoidable congestion at that port’s queue. 
Current proposals for multi-path load balancing in UET, such as REPS, use the presence or absence of 
ECN marks on returning transport ACKs as an indication of whether a packet encountered congestion 
from a flow collision when sent with a particular EV. By efficiently informing sending FEPs of the 
minimum available bandwidth along a packet’s path, the min(ABW) CSIG signal may allow sending FEPs 
to perform a finer-grained comparison of the merits of the paths selected by distinct EVs, such that a 
sending FEP may be able not only to avoid EVs that have experienced congestion, but also to select an 
EV preferentially for a path that offers comparatively more available bandwidth than other EVs’ paths. 
4.2.9.2 min(ABW/C) 
The min(ABW/C) signal is similar to the min(ABW) signal, but its signal value is normalized by the output 
port capacity C in bytes per second. Both the unquantized and quantized normalized available 
bandwidth values are represented as integers. 
In fabrics with heterogeneous port speeds, comparison of absolute available bandwidths measured at 
ports of different speeds does not reliably reveal which port is operating at a greater fraction of its 
capacity. Normalizing by capacity reliably identifies the port operating at the greatest fraction of its 
capacity, even when comparing across ports of different speeds.
