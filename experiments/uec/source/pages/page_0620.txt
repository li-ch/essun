4.2.8 CSIG Traffic Rate Measurement Interval 
The CSIG ABW and ABW/C signal types involve the measurement of a traffic rate. Such a measurement 
is taken over some time interval. Because congestion control algorithms at end systems typically 
consume congestion signals measured at path-RTT granularity, CSIG-capable devices SHOULD choose an 
interval for traffic-rate measurement that is no longer than the unloaded RTT for the shortest-
propagation-delay path within the fabric. 
Comparisons of traffic rates measured at distinct switches and carried in CSIG tags are valid when 
switches along a CSIG-tagged packet’s path use the same measurement interval. Toward ensuring that 
all switches along a path support at least one common measurement interval, a CSIG-capable switch 
SHOULD support a measurement time interval t of 102.4 µs and default to this value at boot. Further, a 
CSIG-capable switch SHOULD support as many values as an implementation permits of the 
measurement time interval t drawn from the range of values [128 ns, 1024 ms] with an increment of 
128 ns. A switch SHOULD support the same measurement intervals for both the ABW and ABW/C signal 
types and MUST configure the same measurement intervals across all switches in the fabric. 
A switch constrained by its arithmetic capabilities can choose to approximate the time interval. In such 
cases the time interval MUST be within the bounds of loss of precision during quantization. 
Implementation Note: 
For signals that measure a traffic rate (e.g., ABW and ABW/C), implementors should take note of the 
interaction between the spacing in time of CSIG-tagged packets within a flow and the time interval 
over which a device measures a traffic rate. For CSIG-tagged packets within a flow to obtain 
representative traffic rate measurements that cover traffic dynamics over time, it is essential they 
arrive at a device at least once per the measurement interval in use by that device. If the gap between
