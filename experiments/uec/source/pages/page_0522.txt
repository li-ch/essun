3.6.18.4 Congestion Signals  
Congestion signals are key metrics gathered either from the network or directly at the endpoint to 
inform congestion management decisions. These signals provide critical information regarding current 
network conditions and performance. For instance, incoming acknowledgment (ACK) packets contain 
service time information and an indication that the associated request was explicit congestion 
notification (ECN) marked. Additionally, endpoint time measurements can be utilized to measure round-
trip time (RTT) for each packet, offering insight into latency variations and network responsiveness. 
Updates to such congestion signals often occur at a higher frequency than the invocation of the CC or LB 
algorithm itself. As a result, signals may undergo multiple updates before being processed, with 
accumulated values maintained according to predefined rules such as summation or maximum 
aggregation. This ensures that the CC and LB algorithms operate on an appropriately synthesized set of 
data, allowing for accurate congestion control adjustments while maintaining computational efficiency. 
Ultra Ethernet categorizes congestion signals into three distinct types: mandatory signals, optional 
signals, and vendor-specific signals. Mandatory signals form the core set that all PCM implementations 
MUST support to ensure baseline compatibility and functionality across deployments. Optional signals 
provide additional capabilities that MAY be implemented selectively based on specific requirements, 
allowing flexibility in adapting congestion management behavior to different use cases. Vendor-specific 
signals MAY be used to enable further extensibility, permitting individual vendors to introduce 
customized metrics tailored to proprietary networking solutions. While the mandatory signals ensure 
interoperability across all UE implementations, optional and vendor-specific signals offer adaptability to 
specialized workloads and infrastructure configurations. Any UE PCM implementation MUST support the 
default signal set, MAY include any subset of the optional signals, and vendors MAY extend the available 
signal set with their own enhancements. 
Signals are configured through a YAML definition specified at algorithm load time as outlined in Section 
3.6.18.8.5. 
3.6.18.4.1 Signal Accumulation Operators 
PCM defines a set of accumulation operations for processing congestion signals. These operations 
determine how multiple signal updates are combined over time. The specific accumulation functions 
used in UE are formally specified in Table 3-106, providing a structured approach to handling signal 
updates within the congestion management framework. Any implementation MUST support all defined 
accumulation operations. Table 3-110 lists mandatory and default accumulators for all signals. 
Table 3-106 - Mandatory Signal and Global Variable Accumulation Operators 
Accumulation Operation Description 
uet_op_sum Running sum 
uet_op_max Running maximum 
uet_op_min Running minimum 
uet_op_last Simply store last update (overwrite)
